# Evidence Policy

Use this policy before scoring or recommending an opportunity.

## Evidence hierarchy

Rank evidence by its ability to show real behavior:

1. **Commitment and behavior:** paid orders, signed contracts, retained usage, completed switching, deposits, renewals, or measured workflow loss.
2. **Operational records:** support tickets, product analytics, procurement data, job postings, budgets, public filings, or repeatable transaction data.
3. **Independent research:** credible third-party studies with a clear method, date, population, and limitations.
4. **Stated intent:** interviews, surveys, anecdotes, and social posts. Useful for language and hypotheses, not proof of demand.
5. **Narrative claims:** founder assertions, uncited market estimates, trend pieces, and model output. Treat as leads only.

Prefer recent, direct, independently checkable evidence. A weaker source can still be useful when it is explicitly labeled and does not carry the conclusion alone.

## Claim ledger

For every material claim, record:

| Claim | Type | Source or basis | Strength | Contradiction | Unknown or next check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A concise factual or causal claim | Evidence, hypothesis, or inference | Citation, provided material, or observation | High, medium, or low | Evidence against it | What would change confidence |

Keep evidence, inference, and recommendation distinct. Label an unsupported statement as a hypothesis or unknown; do not upgrade it because it sounds plausible.

## Contradictions and gates

Seek at least one plausible disconfirming explanation for the problem, target segment, and willingness to pay. Explain whether conflicting sources differ by date, segment, method, or incentives. If a contradiction cannot be resolved, lower confidence and make the experiment test it.

Do not issue `GO` on narrative evidence alone. A `GO` requires strong behavioral or operational evidence plus a credible path to payment. Without access to current sources, say which claims were not verified and avoid treating them as facts.
